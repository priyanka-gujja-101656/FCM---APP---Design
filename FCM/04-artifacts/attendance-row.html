Five facts, one row

Row structure is settled from your reference: avatar with the status dot, the name, then the time and who dropped them off on one quiet line \u2014 no "In" \u2014 and the allergy as an outlined tag beneath. The action sits on the right.

Rows A to C settle the note. Row D adds the overflow you pointed at, and the last frame is the bottom sheet it opens. Scroll sideways.

What is fixed now

Time reads as a time and nothing else \u2014 "8:05 AM \u00b7 Dad". The allergy tag is an outlined pill under the subtitle, warm red for a food allergy and neutral for medication, so severity is visible without a second glance.

One thing I did not copy

Your reference tints the avatar blue for a child who is in. Blue means interactive everywhere else in this app, and the green dot already says "here", so the avatar stays neutral. Two signals for one fact is how the old screen got loud.

What I would ship

Row C. A note exists to be read, and the only version where it is actually read is the one that shows it. Seventeen rows stay three tiers tall; two grow. That difference is the point, not a cost.

The overflow, kept honest

The ellipsis earns its place — mark absent, relocate and add note have nowhere else to live on the row. But it is a glyph, not a button: no border, no fill, so it reads as a handle beside Check out rather than a rival to it.

What the sheet drops

Check Out, because the row already carries it 40px to the left. Five stacked pill buttons also become four list rows, each with a line saying what it does — that marking absent confirms, that moving checks the ratio, that a note expires at midnight. Say the word if you want Check Out kept for muscle memory.

If the register must stay even

Take Row B. Both exceptions become tags in the same place, so every row is the same height and the eye still finds them \u2014 you just have to tap to find out what the note says.
